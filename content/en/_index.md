<h3>History</h3>
<p class="jst">I am preoccupied mostly by the history of the Romanians and of the so-called *(South-)East-European* space (See <a href="https://serbandrugas.github.io/serban-george-paul-drugas/en/blog/" style="color: #4a323c; margin: 0 10px; font-weight: bold;">Blog</a>).</p>
<p class="jst"><b>Dacia, Gotthia, Gepidia, Wallachia, Romania.</b> Interesting all the regions with these names, north or south of the Danube.</p>
<p class="jst">So, the historic study of the Lower Danube basin and neighboring areas gives, for certain, many satisfaction to the researcher. A rich historical space, in any era, starting with the Paleolithic traces to present.</p> 
<p class="jst">The study is, sometimes, shaddowed by the extreme approaches, ideologically influenced. The communist period occurred, for all the countries in the *Central* and *Eastern* Europe, not long after the convulsions determined by the two World Wars. The First, on one hand, collapsed the Austro-Ungarian and the Ottoman Empires, and on the other hand, it created the nation-states. It had many positive outcoms, but it also provoked interminable discussions about how much territory would belong *exactly* to each nation-state. <b>All</b> the nations in the region (and not only, in Europe, but I said on which area I will focus) had protocronist approaches. Each had a *glorious past* to claim. (See <b>Blog</b> why for ALL the nations this is only a propagandistic lie. Not that there wouldn't be a glorious past, but...) We could identify the following extremes, for the history of the Romanians: <b>protocronism</b> -- <b>identitary dissolution</b>. Isn't that so, that we knew only about protocronism? In an unballanced way, it was produced among the Romanian historians, a phobia towards only one extreme, while we barely could name the other. It took me a few seconds to propose the expression *identitary dissolution* for it. Not that the expression wouldn't exist. It defines better the problem. Many Romanian historians became so scared to be considered protocronists, that they manifest a repulsion to present, with ballance, anything positive in the history of the Romanian. How far they went, for instance, in the discussions over the mission of St. Andrew in Dobruja, or, at least in the *formation area of the Romanian people*. We are also afraid to define the later mentioned area in its rightful and complete vastity, north <b>and</b> [this is the key conjunction!] south of the Danube, <i>and not just a stripe corresoonding to the meadow of the great river</i>. (I will approach these problems on the <b>Blog</b>. See, also, my articles.)</p>

<h3>Linguistics</h3>
<p class="jst">I am more interested in the Thracian language (and subclades - Dacian, and adjacent domains - Illyrian, Phrygian) and the Thracian substrate of the Romanian (and Albanian). With all the difficulty of the domain and the controversies (often passionate), we have a lot to learn from: W. Tomaschek, D. Detschew, I. I. Russu, even V. Georgiev, also R. Katičić, I. Duridanov, S. Paliga, etc. See also my articles in Hiperboreea Journal in the field. Not everything is a controversy. Things are also settling down.</p>

<h3>Spirituality</h3>
<p class="jst">MY spirituality is Christian Orthodoxy. It is the only Orthodoxy, because the term was invented inside it, then abusively applied elsewhere. At some point, the Christians who confessed the Nicaeo-Constantinopolitan Creed stated about themselves that they confess justly the faith in God. One of the fundamental principles of our belief is, however, *Judge not, that ye be not judged.* (Matthew 7,1). Isn't it? Thus, we confess a belief, which we can't accept to be relativized. Any believer, of any belief, believes absolutely. Or he/she doesn't (really) believe. What would be the solution not to get to harm each other? It was given by Christ, and I have just iterated it: *Judge not, that ye be not judged*. Period. Meaning that we don't judge the person, for any person is created by the same God, thus pricelessly priced by Him. We are, thus, indebted to judge the beliefs, practices and opinions, function to our belief, because otherwise it would mean we would relativize our own belief. The hard core Orthodoxy starts from here. We don't judge, but love the person, but we state, with arguments, our own belief. It also has less known sources, in its Tradition. I will try to post here as I can, also from these *arcana* of our Tradition. Will them be a challange? Let them be! At some point we would need to wean ourselves and let aside the "milk", if we consider ourselves Christians. E. g., *12 For when for the time ye ought to be teachers, ye have need that one teach you again which be the first principles of the oracles of God; and are become such as have need of milk, and not of strong meat. 13 For every one that useth milk is unskilful in the word of righteousness: for he is a babe. 14 But strong meat belongeth to them that are of full age, even those who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil.* (Hebrews 5,12-14). *1 And I, brethren, could not speak unto you as unto spiritual, but as unto carnal, even as unto babes in Christ. 2 I have fed you with milk, and not with meat: for hitherto ye were not able to bear it, neither yet now are ye able. 3 For ye are yet carnal: for whereas there is among you envying, and strife, and divisions, are ye not carnal, and walk as men?* (1 Corinteni 3,1-3).</p>
